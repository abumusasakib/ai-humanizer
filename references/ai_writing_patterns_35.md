# 35 AI Writing Patterns

Source: [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer), itself drawing on Wikipedia's
WikiProject AI Cleanup list of AI writing indicators. Check text against these before rewriting.

## Content Patterns

1. **Inflated claims about importance and legacy** — "stands/serves as," "pivotal moment," "marking a shift" overstate ordinary details.
2. **Name-dropping to prove importance** — lists publications or follower counts without context, to fake significance.
3. **Shallow analysis with -ing phrases** — gerund tags like "symbolizing..." make plain facts sound deeper than they are.
4. **Sales language** — "nestled," "vibrant," "stunning," "breathtaking" — creates an advertisement tone.
5. **Vague sources** — attributes claims to unnamed "experts," "observers," or "industry reports" instead of naming a real source.
6. **Formulaic challenges/outlook sections** — stock "Despite challenges..." or "Future Outlook" sections repeat vague claims with no facts.

## Language and Grammar Patterns

7. **Overused AI words** — "delve," "intricate," "landscape," "testament," "underscore," "showcase."
8. **Avoiding "is"/"are"** — "serves as," "boasts," "features" in place of a plain "is" or "has."
9. **"Not X but Y" and clipped negative endings** — overused "Not only...but..." and fragments like "no guessing."
10. **Forced groups of three** — ideas artificially bundled into triads for manufactured completeness.
11. **Renaming the subject / repeated sentence openings** — swapping in synonyms for the same subject repeatedly, or starting many sentences the same way.
12. **False "from X to Y" ranges** — used when the two endpoints don't form a real progression.
13. **Passive voice / missing subjects** — hides who is doing the acting.

## Style Patterns

14. **Em and en dashes** — no `—` or `–` in the final text, unless the user's own writing sample uses them.
15. **Excessive bold text** — bolding words with no clear reason.
16. **Bold mini-headings in lists** — every list item opens with a bold label and colon for no reason.
17. **Title Case In Headings** — capitalizing every main word instead of sentence case.
18. **Decorative emojis** — added to headings/list items with no function.
19. **Curly quotation marks** — used where the format calls for straight quotes.

## Chatbot Patterns

20. **Leftover chatbot text** — "I hope this helps," "let me know," "Want me to..." left in standalone prose.
21. **Knowledge-limit disclaimers / confident guessing** — "As of my last training..." or filling gaps with a guess presented as fact.
22. **Overly agreeable tone** — praising the user or agreeing before answering: "Great question! You're absolutely right."

## Filler and Hedging

23. **Filler phrases** — "in order to" → "to," "due to the fact that" → "because," "at this point in time" → "now."
24. **Stacked qualifiers** — "could potentially possibly might arguably" undercuts every claim.
25. **Generic positive endings** — "The future looks bright," "Exciting times lie ahead."
26. **Over-hyphenation** — "cross-functional," "high-quality," "data-driven" applied everywhere, even after the noun.
27. **Fake deeper-truth framing** — "The real question is," "at its core," "what really matters" inflate an ordinary point.
28. **Announcing the next point instead of making it** — "Let's dive in," "here's what you need to know."
29. **Heading restated as the first sentence** — a one-line paragraph that just repeats the heading before any real content.
30. **Describing a previous/outdated version** — talks about old behavior instead of current behavior, outside a changelog.
31. **Forced punchlines / dramatic fragments** — every sentence staged as a closing line; rows of short fragments for effect.
32. **Formulaic sayings** — "X is the Y of Z," "becomes a trap," "is not a tool but a mirror" — sounds deep, says nothing specific.
33. **Fake-candid openings** — "Honestly?" "Look," "Here's the thing" used as a theatrical hook before a routine point.
34. **Answering objections no one raised** — "This isn't mainly about...," "I'm not arguing that..." defends against an unstated position.
35. **Rejecting fake alternatives** — introduces an unlikely option, rejects it, and never mentions it again.

## Non-negotiables while rewriting

- Every claim survives the rewrite — trim dull parts, expand thin ones, but don't drop information.
- No invented facts: names, numbers, dates, quotes, and citations must trace back to the source or the user.
- Code, data, and frontmatter are left untouched — only prose gets rewritten.
- If the user supplies a writing sample, mirror its rhythm, word choice, and punctuation instead of applying the default style rules.
